01 — Vision & scope

An informative front door with a working school behind it

Today, nadagurukulam.org introduces the gurukulam — its founder's message, vision, the six disciplines taught (Carnatic vocal, Hindustani vocal, Bharatanatyam, Mridangam, Tabla, Flute), and its faculty. The rebuild keeps that role and adds a second one: everything the school actually runs on — curriculum, timetables, batches, lesson plans, live classes, assignments, feedback and UGC compliance documents — moves onto the same platform, behind a single login, editable by you first.

Every visible piece of the public site — text, images, sections, menus, the events calendar, job listings — is a record in the database, not a line of code. Changing it is a Super Admin action, not a developer request. That is the meaning of "completely dynamic" for this build: nothing on the public pages is hardcoded once launched.

06 — Dynamic CMS approach

What "editable by Super Admin first" actually means

Four working rules make the public site behave like software the school owns, rather than a template a developer maintains.

01

Content blocks, not fixed pages

Every public page is assembled from reusable, editable blocks — hero, text section, image gallery, staff grid, event list. Adding a new About sub-section is a Super Admin action, not a deploy.

02

Draft, preview, publish

Edits save as a draft first. You preview exactly what a visitor will see, then publish — so the live site never shows half-finished work.

03

One media library

Photos, videos and documents are uploaded once and reused across the public site, events, and academic modules — no duplicate uploads per page.

04

Design tokens, centrally set

Colours, type scale and logo live in one settings screen, mapped to the brand system below — so a palette or font change applies sitewide at once.

07 — Technical architecture

Vercel, Render, MongoDB, Supabase, GitHub

Your stack choices map cleanly onto the two kinds of data this system holds: structured, permissioned records (Supabase/Postgres) and flexible, block-shaped content (MongoDB). One change from your original list: Vercel in place of Netlify for the frontend.

Both have a free tier and deploy straight from GitHub, but the public site needs pages that stay fresh without a full rebuild — new events, a new enquiry count on Overview, a newly published page. That's server-rendering and incremental regeneration, which Next.js does natively and Vercel (built by the same team) runs on its free Hobby tier without extra configuration: 100GB bandwidth/month, serverless functions included, and an automatic preview link for every change before it goes live. Netlify can run Next.js too, but through an adapter layer, with edge/SSR features gated behind paid tiers sooner. If unlimited bandwidth matters more than SSR convenience, Cloudflare Pages is the other strong free option — it just asks for the next-on-pages adapter and is a touch more hands-on to configure. Vercel is the better default for this build; either swaps in without changing anything else below.

10 — Decisions & follow-ups

Settled so far, and what's next

Six decisions in, all shaped around the same constraint: the institution runs entirely on donations and offers everything free of cost, so every choice below defaults to whatever keeps running cost at or near zero without weakening security.

✓
Students get their own portal loginConfirmed — a Student role joins the six staff-side roles, scoped to Timetable, Live Classes, Assignments and Feedback.
✓
This is a full rebuild, not an add-onConfirmed — nadagurukulam.org's current pages become dynamic, CMS-driven pages on the new stack, with the portal built alongside.
✓
Live classes: embed, don't build — Jitsi Meet as the default

Building in-house video means running media servers and TURN/STUN infrastructure indefinitely — an ongoing engineering and hosting cost a donation-funded institution shouldn't carry. Embedding is the right call.

Recommended default: Jitsi Meet — open-source, free with no time or participant limits, and embeddable directly inside the Live Classes module via its iframe API, so nobody ever leaves the portal. It can be self-hosted later at zero licence cost if you want a fully branded room.

For larger one-to-many events (assemblies, guest lectures), apply for Google for Nonprofits — as a registered charitable mission you're very likely eligible, and it unlocks free Google Workspace with Google Meet (higher participant caps, recordings to Drive, calendar sync) at no cost. Meet doesn't embed as cleanly as Jitsi, so treat it as an "open in a new tab" option for big events rather than the everyday classroom tool. Zoom's free tier caps group calls at 40 minutes and its nonprofit rate is discounted, not free — so it's the fallback, not the default.

✓
Documents: Supabase + MongoDB now — a cheaper, safer plan for sensitive files as you grow

Confirmed for now: Supabase Storage holds the actual files, MongoDB holds their metadata and access rules. Two things to plan ahead for as the library of student, staff and UGC documents grows — storage cost, and how sensitive some of these files are.

Cost, once you outgrow Supabase's free storage: Cloudflare R2 or Backblaze B2 are the cheapest online options — S3-compatible object storage around $0.015/GB-month, and unlike AWS S3 or most providers, neither charges for downloads. That matters here, since students and staff will be pulling documents constantly. The backend keeps issuing short-lived, permission-checked signed URLs, so the access-control model doesn't change — only where the bytes physically live.

Safety, for the most sensitive records (ID proof, medical notes, anything involving minors): keep an "online/hot" encrypted copy in object storage for everyday access, plus a periodic encrypted offline backup — an external drive or NAS at the institution, synced on a schedule — so those records survive even if a cloud account is ever compromised or locked out. Alongside that: encryption at rest and in transit, an access log of who viewed or downloaded each document, collecting the minimum personal data needed, and — since some students may be minors — the extra care India's data-protection rules expect around children's information. We'll design this in full in Phase 4; nothing about today's Supabase/MongoDB choice needs to change to get there.

✓
English at launch, Indian languages ready to addConfirmed — English only for launch. The CMS content-block model (Phase 2/4) carries a locale field from day one, so Kannada, Hindi and Sanskrit versions of public-site content can be added per page or section later without restructuring anything. The portal itself (admin/teacher/student side) stays English-only unless you say otherwise.
✓
Domain: test on a free subdomain now, move to nadagurukulam.org once you have access

Since neither domain is in your control yet, build and demo on the free subdomains the hosts already give you: Vercel issues something like nada-gurukulam.vercel.app for the frontend, and Render gives a matching .onrender.com URL for the API — both live immediately, at zero cost, which is exactly what you need to show your admin for approval.

Moving to the real domain later is then a one-time DNS change — point nadagurukulam.org at Vercel and Render — with no rebuild required. The site your admin approves on the .vercel.app link is the exact site that goes live on the real domain.
