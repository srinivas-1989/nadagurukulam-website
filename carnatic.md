1.  **CARNATIC VOCAL THEORY**

**Program Outcomes (POs)**

Upon completion of the 4-year BPA program, students will be able to:

- **PO1:** Demonstrate mastery over the theoretical and practical
  aspects of Carnatic music.

- **PO2:** Analyze and interpret ancient musical treatises in a modern
  context.

- **PO3:** Perform complex *Manodharma* (improvisational) aspects with
  creative and technical precision.

- **PO4:** Conduct independent research in musicology using standardized
  methodologies.

- **PO5:** Qualify for competitive academic exams like UGC NET/JRF for
  higher research.

- **Total Credits:** 3

- **Total Semester Hours:** 45 Hours

- **Distribution:** 4 Modules per Semester

### **Semester 1: Module-wise Detailed Syllabus**

***Module 1: Musical Terminology & Sangeetha Lakshana***

- **The Origin of Sound:** Study of *Nada*---classification into *Ahata*
  (man-made/struck) and *Anahata* (natural/unstruck) sounds;  the three
  levels of sound intensity (*Mandhra, Madhya, Tara*).

- **Shruti & Swara:** Definition & introduction of the 22 Shrutis
  (lineage of pitch/microtones) and *the 12 Swarasthanas (semitones).*

- **Swara nomenclature:** Detailed study of the 12 *Swarasthanas* and
  the 16 names (*Shodasa Swara*); system used in Carnatic music to
  identify variants of Ri, Ga, Dha, and Ni.

- **Sthayi & Laya:** Understanding *Sthayi* (octave), and the three
  speeds of rhythm *Laya* (tempo; Prathama, Dwitiya, Tritiya), and the
  concept of *Adhara Shadjam*.

**Course Outcomes -- CO1 RBT Levels -- L1, L2**

***Module 2: The 72 Melakarta Scheme***

- **Logic of the System:** Introduction to the 72 parent ragas as
  codified by Venkatamakhin.

- **The Katapayadi Sankhya:** The Sanskrit alphanumeric code used to
  determine a raga\'s number and notes.

- **The Chakra System:** Classification of the 72 ragas into 12
  *Chakras* (groups of 6), such as *Indu, Netra, Agni,* etc. Suddha &
  Prati Madhyama ragas - The division of the scheme into the first 36
  (Purva) and the latter 36 (Uttara) Ragas.

- **Vivadi Swaras:** Theoretical understanding of ragas containing
  dissonant intervals and their placement in the scheme.

**Course Outcomes -- CO2 RBT Levels -- L3, L4**

***Module 3: Raga Classification (Janaka & Janya)***

- **Parent and Derivative Ragas:** The characteristics of a \"Parent\"
  Raga (Must be Sampurna, have same notes in ascent/descent etc.),
  relationship between *Janaka* (parent) and *Janya* (descendant) ragas.

- **Janya Raga Sub-types:** \* *Varja Ragas* (omitted notes).

  - *Vakra Ragas* (Non-linear or zig-zag scales).

  - *Upanga and Bhashanga Ragas that stay strictly within the parent
    notes versus those that introduce \"foreign\" notes (Anya Swara).*

  - The Concept of Anya Swara: How specific compositions introduce notes
    outside the parent scale for aesthetic beauty.

- **Ghanaraga Panchakam:** Introduction and theoretical overview of the
  five "Ghana" (heavy) ragas (*Nata, Gaula, Arabhi, Varali, Sri*)
  traditionally used for deep improvisation.

**Course Outcomes -- CO3 RBT Levels -- L2, L4**

***Module 4: Early Compositional Forms***

- **Abhyasa Gana:** The educational repertoire of Carnatic music. The
  sequence of learning---Sarali Varisais, Jantai, Alankaram.

- **Gitam:** Structural analysis of the simplest melodic form; The first
  melodic composition; study of Pillari and Sanchari Gitams; structural
  study of Lakshana Gitams (which describe raga grammar) and Lakshya
  Gitams.

- **Jatiswaram and Swarajati:** Analysis of forms that blend rhythm
  (*Jatis*) with melody (*Swaras*); the transition from technical
  exercises to performance pieces. Distinguishing between forms that are
  purely melodic (Jatiswaram) and those that transitioned into dance and
  storytelling (Swarajati).

- **Notation Symbols:** Introduction to basic Solfa notation: signs for
  *Aksharakala*, pauses (*Anudrutam*), and octave dots.

**Course Outcomes -- CO4 RBT Levels -- L2, L5**

**Methodology for Semester 1:**

- **Mathematical Drills:** Using the Katapayadi formula to calculate
  Raga numbers.

- **Comparative Listening:** Identifying the difference between a
  *Sampurna* (complete) and *Varja* (incomplete) raga.

- **Notation Training:** Writing basic Gitams in the Indian Solfa
  system.

**Learning Resources**

- Reference Books: The Spiritual Heritage of Tyagaraja by C.

### **Suggested Activities**

2.  **Raga Mapping:** Creating a \"Family Tree\" chart for a specific

3.  **Form Analysis:** Listening to a *Gitam* and a *Swarajati* and

### **Semester 2: Module-wise Detailed Syllabus**

#### **Module 1: The Art and Evolution of South Indian Notation System**

- **Notation Symbols:** Mastery of symbols for different octaves (dots
  above/below), duration (commas, semicolons), and speed divisions.
  Symbols for first, second, and third speeds (Prathama, Dwitiya,
  Tritiya Kalam).

- **Structural Notating:** Techniques for notating a Swaravali or a
  Gitam with correct rhythmic subdivisions. Techniques for writing
  **Varnams** and **Kritis** including Pallavi, Anupallavi, and Charanam
  demarcations.

- **Gamaka Representation:** Traditional and modern ways of indicating
  oscillations (*Kampita*), slides (*Jaru*), and stress (*Spuritham*) in
  text.

- **Comparative Scripting:** A look at how notation has evolved from
  palm-leaf manuscripts to the standardized modern system.
  Bhatkhande-style Influence; Adapting symbols for different octaves
  (*Sthayis*) and rhythmic pulses (*Akshara Kalas*).

**Course Outcomes -- CO1 RBT Levels -- L3, L6**

#### **Module 2: Pitamaha & Early Composers**

- **Purandara Dasa:** The "Pitamaha" (Grandfather) of Carnatic music;
  his role in standardization of the grading system (Sarali to
  Alankaram); creating the *Abhyasa Gana* (foundational exercises); his
  social philosophy and the "Suladi" format.

- **Tallapaka Annamacharya:** The pioneer of the *Sankirtana* tradition;
  structural analysis of Pallavi-Charanam format in his *Adhyatma* and
  *Sringara* compositions and his role in the Venkateswara temple
  tradition.

- **Dasa Sahitya:** The spiritual and social philosophy behind the
  *Devaranamas*. How Kanaka Dasa and other used the "Ugabhoga" and
  "Devaranama" to bridge the gap between classical and folk devotion.

- **Bhadrachala Ramadasu:** His influence on the *Kirtana* form and the
  development of devotional music.

**Course Outcomes -- CO2 RBT Levels -- L2, L4**

#### **Module 3: Ancient Tamil Musicology (Silappadikaram)**

Exploring the non-Sanskritic roots of South Indian music through Tamil
literature.

- **The Epic of the Anklet:** Contextual study of music and dance in
  Ilango Adigal's *Silappadikaram*.

- **The Pan System:** Study of ancient Tamil *Pans* and their
  equivalence to modern Ragas (e.g., *Sempalaipalai* to *Harikambhoji*).

- **Vatta-Palai & Yazh:** Understanding the ancient circular mapping of
  notes and the evolution of the *Yazh* (ancient harp) into the Veena.

- **Kural, Thutham, Kaikkilai:** Nomenclature of the seven notes in the
  ancient Tamil tradition.

**Course Outcomes -- CO3 RBT Levels -- L4, L5**

#### **Module 4: Voice Culture & Physiology**

- **Vocal Anatomy:** The science of the larynx, the lungs, vocal cords,
  the role of the diaphragm in breath support, and the sinus cavities in
  resonance and pitch production.

- **Sadhakam (Practice) Techniques/Breathing Mechanics:** Theoretical
  study of diaphragmatic breathing and breath-holding (Kumbhaka) for
  long phrases (Karvai). Traditional methods for developing "Sharira"
  (voice quality), flexibility, and volume control across three octaves,
  methods for developing *Akara* (vowel clarity), *Bhruka* (humming),
  and *Humkara*.

- **Acoustics of the Voice -- Range and Flexibility:** Understanding how
  vowel sounds (Akara, Ikara, Ukara) change the shape of the oral cavity
  and affect tonal colour. Exercises to increase vocal reach across
  three octaves (*Tristhayi*).

- **Vocal Health:** Ayurvedic and modern perspectives on maintaining the
  voice and preventing strain.

**Course Outcomes -- CO4 RBT Levels -- L3**

### **Methodology for Semester 2**

- **Notation Scripting Lab:** Converting 17th-century palm-leaf style

- **Comparative Charting:** Mapping ancient Tamil *Pans* to their

- **Lexical Mapping:** Creating a \"Tamil-Sanskrit Music Glossary\" to

- **Transcription Workshops:** Converting a recorded melodic phrase into

- **Dictation Drills:** The teacher sings a phrase, and students must
  correctly notate it with octave and speed markings.

- **Biographical Seminars:** Student-led discussions on the impact of
  Purandara Dasa's standardized teaching system. Comparing the lyrical
  styles of Purandara Dasa and Annamacharya.

## **Semester 3: Tala Dasa Pranas & Rhythmic Geometry**

### 

### **Course Objectives (Cob)**

- **Cob 1:** To analyze the ten vital elements (**Tala Dasa Pranas**)
  that govern the life and movement of rhythm.

- **Cob 2:** To master the mathematical logic of the **35 Tala Scheme**
  through the permutation of Suladi Sapta Talas and Jati variations.

- **Cob 3:** To understand the concept of **Graha (Eduppu)** and its
  role in the aesthetic placement of a composition within a rhythmic
  cycle.

- **Cob 4:** To explore the visual and structural patterns of **Yati**
  and their application in sophisticated melodic and rhythmic designs.

### 

### **Course Outcomes (CO)**

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- **CO 1:** Evaluate the structural integrity of any Tala based on the
  Dasa Prana framework.

- **CO 2:** Calculate and demonstrate the total Akshara Kalas of any
  Tala within the 35-Tala system.

- **CO 3:** Identify and execute different Eduppus (Grahas) in varied
  compositions to maintain rhythmic equilibrium.

- **CO 4:** Design rhythmic patterns using different Yati structures
  (Gopucha, Srotovaha, etc.) in both melody and rhythm.

### **Pedagogy**

- **Mathematical Workshops:** Step-by-step derivation of Tala counts
  using Jati and Gati permutations.

- **Rhythmic Visualization:** Using diagrams to map Yati patterns and
  Graha placements.

- **Practical Drills:** Hands-on training in keeping "Thalam" while
  executing complex "Eduppus."

- **Comparative Analysis:** Studying different compositions to see how
  "Pranas" like *Laya* and *Marga* are applied.

### 

### **Semester 3: Module-wise Detailed Syllabus**

#### **Module 1: The Tala Dasa Pranas**

- **The Ten Life-Breaths:** Detailed study of the five physical and five
  mental aspects of Tala: *Kaala, Marga, Kriya, Anga, Graha, Jati,
  Kalai, Laya, Yati,* and *Prastara*.

- **Kaala & Marga:** Understanding the units of time and the "pathway"
  of the rhythm (e.g., *Dakshina, Vartika, Chitra*).

- **Kriya:** Classification into *Nishabda* (silent) and *Sashabda*
  (sounded) movements of the hand.

- **Angas:** Beyond the Shadangas---understanding the role of
  *Anudhrutam, Dhrutam, Laghu, Guru, Plutam,* and *Kakapadam*.

**Course Outcomes -- CO1** **RBT Levels -- L2, L5**

#### 

#### **Module 2: The 35 Tala Scheme & Jati Variations**

- **The Suladi Sapta Talas:** Definition and structure of the seven
  basic talas: *Dhruva, Matya, Rupaka, Jhampa, Triputa, Ata,* and *Eka*.

- **Jati Permutations:** The impact of changing the *Laghu* value
  (*Trisra, Chaturasra, Khanda, Misra, Sankeerna*) on the seven talas to
  create the 35-tala matrix.

- **The 175 Tala Concept:** A theoretical introduction to how the 35
  talas expand into 175 through the application of the five *Gatis*
  (pulses).

- **Practical Calculation:** Calculating the total *Matra* and *Akshara*
  counts for complex talas like *Misra Jati Jhampa Tala* or *Khanda Jati
  Ata Tala*.

**Course Outcomes -- CO2** **RBT Levels -- L3, L4**

#### 

#### **Module 3: The Concept of Graha (Eduppu) & Its Types**

- **Definition of Graha:** The starting point of the song (Sahitya) in
  relation to the starting point of the Tala cycle.

- **Sama Graha:** When the song and the tala start simultaneously.

- **Vishama Graha:** Detailed study of

  - **Anagata** (song starts after the tala)

  - **Atita** (song starts before the tala).

- **Mathematical Precision:** Calculating *Eduppu* in terms of *Matras*
  and *Aksharas* for various *Kritis* and *Varnams*.

**Course Outcomes -- CO3** **RBT Levels -- L3, L4**

#### 

#### **Module 4: Yati and Rhythmic Geometry**

- **The Six Yatis:** Structural analysis of rhythmic flow: *Sama,
  Gopucha* (cow's tail), *Srotovaha* (river flow), *Damaru* (hourglass),
  *Mridanga* (drum shape), and *Vishama*.

- **Yati in Sahitya:** Examples from Muthuswami Dikshitar's compositions
  (e.g., *Tyagaraja Yoga Vaibhavam*) where words follow geometric
  patterns.

- **Yati in Kalpanaswaras:** Learning to build rhythmic ending patterns
  (*Korvais*) using *Srotovaha* and *Gopucha* logic.

**Course Outcomes -- CO4** **RBT Levels -- L4, L6**

### **Methodology for Semester 3:**

- **Tala Charting:** Creating a visual matrix of the 35 talas with their
  specific *Anga* notations.

- **Eduppu Drills:** Listening to 10 different compositions and
  identifying whether the *Graha* is Sama or Vishama.

- **Pattern Design:** Students will be asked to write a simple 4-bar
  rhythmic phrase following a *Srotovaha Yati* (gradually increasing
  length).

- **Mnemonic Recitation (Konnakol):** Reciting the *Jatis* of the 35
  talas to internalize the varied *Laghu* lengths.

**Semester 4: Module-wise Detailed Syllabus**

#### **Module 1: The Varnam -- The Master Architect**

- **Structural Anatomy:** Detailed study of *Pallavi, Anupallavi,
  Muktayi Swara, Charanam,* and *Chittaswaras*.

- **Tana vs. Pada Varnam:** Comparing the rhythmic focus and technical
  speed of Tana Varnams with the melodic, emotive, and *Sahitya*-rich
  Pada Varnams used in dance.

- **Pedagogical Significance:** Why the Varnam is considered the
  "Grammar of the Raga"; its role in developing *Gatra Dharma* (voice
  culture) and *Tala* stability.

- **Ghanaraga Panchaka Varnams:** Introduction to the importance of the
  five major ragas through the Varnam format.

**Course Outcomes -- CO1** **RBT Levels -- L4, L5**

#### 

#### **Module 2: The Kirtana & Kriti (Devotional & Artistic Forms)**

- **The Kirtana:** Evolution of the devotional form; focus on *Nama
  Siddhanta* and simple melodic structures; study of Annamayya's
  compositions, the *Divyanama* and *Utsava Sampradaya* Kirtanas.

- **The Kriti:** The peak of "Art Music"; study of the sophisticated
  Pallavi-Anupallavi-Charanam structure; the introduction of *Sangatis*
  (variations) by Tyagaraja.

- **Post-Trinity Innovations:** Study of the *Swara-sahitya,
  Madhyamakala Sahitya,* and *Chittaswaras* added to Kritis to enhance
  complexity.

- **Lyrical Components:** Understanding the role of *Mudra* (composer's
  signature), *Prosody* (Prasa, Yati, Anuprasa), and *Rasa*.

**Course Outcomes -- CO2** **RBT Levels -- L2, L4**

#### **Module 3: Comparative Study: Carnatic vs. Hindustani**

- **Systemic Overlap:** Historical divergence of the two systems;
  identifying common roots in ancient texts (13^th^-14^th^ Century).

- **Scale Systems:**

  - Comparing the **72 Melakarta** (mathematical) system of the South
    with the **10 Thaat** (classification-based) system of the North.

  - Comparative study of major scales: *Sankarabharanam vs. Bilawal,
    Mayamalavagowla vs. Bhairav, Kalyani vs. Yaman, Todi vs. Bhairavi*.

- **Rhythmic Structures:**

  - Detailed study of *Tala* vs. *Taal*;

  - The concept of the *Laghu* (variable) vs. the *Theka* (fixed
    patterns).

- **Performance Aesthetics:**

  - Comparison of the *Katcheri* format vs. the *Khayal* format;

  - The difference between *Gamaka* (oscillations) and *Meend* (glides).

- **Terminology Translation:** Mapping Southern terms to Northern
  equivalents (e.g., *Mela* to *Thaat*, *Tala* to *Taal*, *Sruti* to
  *Sur*).

**Course Outcomes -- CO3** **RBT Levels -- L4, L5**

#### **Module 4: The Grammar of Manodharma & Introduction to Advanced Improvisation**

- **The Five Branches:**

  - Theoretical introduction to *Raga Alapana, Niraval, Kalpanaswara,
    Ragam-Tanam-Pallavi (RTP),* and *Pallavi*.

  - **Ragam-Tanam-Pallavi (RTP) Overview:** Brief theoretical
    introduction to the pinnacle of Manodharma.

- **Alapana Paddhati:** The stages of Raga exploration---*Akshiptika*
  (Introduction), *Ragavardhani* (Expansion), *Makharini* (Conclusion),
  *Vidari,* and *Sthayi*.

- **Kalpanaswaras (Swarakalpana):**

### **Semester 5: Module-wise Detailed Syllabus**

#### **Module 1: Historical Treatises & Lexicons**

- **Ancient Era:** Study of Bharata's *Natya Shastra* (concept of
  *Jaati* and *Gramas*) and Matanga's *Brihaddesi* (the first definition
  of \"Raga\").

- **Medieval Era:** Analysis of Sarngadeva's *Sangita Ratnakara* and
  Ramamatya's *Swaramelakalanidhi* (evolution of the *Mela* concept).

- **Modern Bridge:** Somanatha's *Ragavibodha* and Govinda's *Sangraha
  Chudamani* (the 72 Melakarta scheme refinement).

- **Lexical Evolution:** How the terminology of the *Shruti* and *Swara*
  shifted through these texts over a millennium.

**Course Outcomes -- CO2** **RBT Levels -- L4, L5**

#### **Module 2: Raga Lakshana (Standard Raga Study)**

- **Technical Parameters:** Detailed study of *Arohana-Avarohana*,
  *Varjya/Vakra* patterns, and classification (*Upanga/Bhashanga*).

- **The main Characteristics of a Raga:**

  - Raga Dasa Lakshanas as per Bharata's Natya Sastra (4^th^ Century
    B.C.) and

  - Raga Trayodasa Lakshanas as per the Sarangadeva's Sangita Ratnakara
    (13^th^ Century A.D.).

  - Understanding *Graha, amsa, tara, mandra, nyasa, apanyasa, sanyasa,
    vinyasa, bahutva, alpatva, antara marga, shadava and audava.*

- **Standard Study of Ragas:** Extensive profile building for:

  - **Major Ragas:** *Todi, Sankarabharanam, Kalyani, Bhairavi,
    Kambhoji*.

  - **Janya & Minor Ragas:** *Mohanam, Hamsadhwani, Anandabhairavi,
    Sahana, Ritigowla*.

- **Sanchara Documentation:** Writing characteristic phrases that define
  the *Swaroopa* (personality) of the Raga.

**Course Outcomes -- CO1** **RBT Levels -- L2, L4**

#### 

#### **Module 3: The Trinity of Carnatic Music and the Post-Trinity Era**

- **Tyagaraja:** Mastery of the Kriti format and melodic variations
  (Sangatis); the use of *Sangatis* and the emphasis on *Bhakti* and
  *Rama Nama*. (The *Naukacharitram* and *Prahlada Bhakti Vijayam*.)

- **Muthuswami Dikshitar:** The *Vibhakti Kritis* and *Navagraha*
  series; the *Vainika* style of vocalization and extensive use of
  *Madhyamakala Sahitya*. Raga-Mudras and the *Gopucha/Srotovaha* Yati
  applications.

- **Syama Sastri:** The architect of rhythm; mastery of *Misra Chapu
  Tala* and the *Swarajati* form (*Ratnatrayam*).

- **The Post-Trinity Influence:** Study of the disciples (The *Sishya
  Parampara*) and composers like Swati Tirunal, Patnam Subramania Iyer,
  and Papanasam Sivan.

**Course Outcomes -- CO3** **RBT Levels -- L2, L4**

#### **Module 4: Vaggeyakara Mudras & Signatures**

- **Types of Mudras:** Understanding the different \"stamps\":

  - **Ankita Mudra:** Composer\'s name/pen-name (e.g., *Guruguha,
    Tyagaraja, Syama Krishna*).

  - **Raga Mudra:** Mention of the Raga name within the lyrics.

  - **Tala Mudra:** Mention of the Tala within the composition.

  - **Vageyakara Mudra:** Identifying the specific poetic style unique
    to a composer.

  - **Vamsa/Guru Mudra:** References to their lineage or spiritual
    teacher (e.g., *Guruguha* for Dikshitar).

  - **Kshetra Mudra:** Naming the specific temple or location for which
    the song was composed (e.g., *Kanchi, Srirangam*).

  - **Prabandha Mudra:** Indicators of the song\'s structural type or
    rhythm.

- **Historical Survey:** Signatures of pre-Trinity (Annamacharya,
  Purandara Dasa) versus modern composers (Balamuralikrishna, Lalgudi
  Jayaraman).

- **Significance:** The role of Mudras in authenticating compositions
  and preserving authorship in an oral tradition.

**Course Outcomes -- CO4** **RBT Levels -- L2, L3**

- **Historical Mapping:** Reviewing archival records and oral histories

### **Semester 6: Module-wise Detailed Syllabus**

#### **Module 1: The Modern Concert Era - Evolution & Innovation**

- **The Transition of Patronage:** The shift from the Tanjore and Mysore
  royal courts to the mercantile and intellectual \"Sabhas\" of Madras
  (Chennai).

- **Democratization of Art:** How the transition to public Sabhas
  influenced Raga selection, time management, and the shortening of
  elaborate *Ragam-Tanam-Pallavis*.

- **The Music Academy & Institutionalization:** The role of the 1927
  Madras Music Conference in codifying theory and establishing the
  \"December Season\" as a global cultural event.

- **Musical Acoustics & Innovation:**

  - The physics of the acoustics of modern venues and the evolution of
    the *Sangeetha Sabha* culture.

  - The modern concert hall; sound absorption, reflection, and the
    impact of electronic amplification on vocal \"close-mic\"
    techniques.

  - Evolution from the manual Tambura to electronic Sruti boxes and
    apps; the impact of recording formats (78 RPM records & LP discs to
    digital) on composition length.

  - Modern digital archiving and the role of social media/streaming in
    the global spread of Carnatic music.

**Course Outcomes -- CO1** **RBT Levels -- L2, L4**

#### **Module 2: The Art of Accompaniment & Concert Ethics**

- **The melodic accompaniment's Role:**

  - Techniques of \"following/shadowing\" the vocalist;

  - The ethics of the solo turn in Alapana and Swara; maintaining
    melodic fidelity.

  - Ethics of mimicking vs. complementing the main artist.

- **The percussion accompaniment's Role:**

  - The role of the Mridangam, Ghatam, and Khanjira;

  - Providing rhythmic \"Sarvalaghu\" support;

  - The art of the *Gumki*; anticipation of *Eduppus* and the structure
    of the *Tani Avartanam* (percussion solo).

  - **Ensemble Hierarchy:** Understanding the unspoken cues between the
    lead artist and the \"Upapakkavadyams\" (Ghatam, Kanjira, Morsing).

- **Platform Etiquette:** Stage presence, seating
  arrangements,microphone etiquette, and the mutual respect required for
  a successful collaborative performance.

- **Supporting the Manodharma:** How a melodic accompanists and
  percussionist respond to spontaneous *Alapana* and *Kalpanaswaras*.

**Course Outcomes -- CO2** **RBT Levels -- L3, L5**

#### **Module 3: Concepts of Rasa and Bhava in Performance**

- **Rasa Theory:** Study of the *Nava Rasas* (Nine Emotions) and their
  specific manifestation in Carnatic Ragas (e.g., *Karuna* in Sahana,
  *Veera* in Atana).

- **Bhava (Expression):**

  - The difference between *Vachika* (lyrical) and *Angika*
    (physical/expressive) Bhava;

  - Distinguishing between *Vibhava* (cause), *Anubhava* (physical
    effect), and *Sanchari Bhava* (fleeting emotions) within a song.

  - The role of *Aparoksha Anubhava* (direct experience) of the
    composer.

- **Raga-Rasa Correspondence:**

  - Scientific and psychological theories on why certain intervals evoke
    specific moods.

  - The role of lyrics in evoking emotion; the concept of *Dhwani*
    (suggestive meaning) in the compositions of the Trinity.

- **The Performer-Audience Bridge:**

  - The concept of *Sahridaya* (the empathetic listener) and the
    transfer of emotion from the stage to the gallery.

  - Techniques for using *Gamakas* and *Kaku* (voice modulation) to
    enhance the emotional delivery of a Kriti.

**Course Outcomes -- CO3** **RBT Levels -- L3, L4**

#### **Module 4: Principles of Concert Planning (Katcheri Dharma)**

- **The Ariyakudi Revolution:** In-depth study of **Ariyakudi Ramanuja
  Iyengar** and the standardization of the *Katcheri Paddhati* (the
  modern concert sequence).

- **The Anatomy of a Concert List:**

  - **The Opening:** Selecting a *Varnam* or a brisk *Ganapathi Kriti*
    to establish the *Sruti* and tempo.

  - **The Middle (Main/Sub-main):** Balancing heavy Ragas (*Todi,
    Kalyani*) with medium-paced pieces.

  - **The Pinnacle:** Theoretical placement of the *Ragam-Tanam-Pallavi*
    (RTP).

  - **The Conclusion:** *Thukkadas* (lighter pieces), *Padams, Jawalis,
    Tillanas,* and the *Mangalam*.

- **Repertoire Selection:**

  - Balancing \"Heavy\" (Ghanam) Ragas with \"Pratimadhyama\" Ragas;

  - Principles of selecting compositions in different *Talas* (beats),
    *Eduppus* (starting points), and *Gatis* (rhythmic pulses) to avoid
    monotony and different composers to ensure variety.

  - **Time Management:**

    - Adapting a concert list for a 60-minute, 90-minute, or 3-hour
      slot;

    - The \"Golden Ratio\" of Manodharma vs. Kalpita music.

- **Katcheri Dharma (Ethics):**

  - Mutual respect between the vocalist and the instrumentalists.

  - The art of providing space for the *Tani Avartanam* (percussion
    solo).

  - Understanding the audience profile and the occasion (Temple,
    Wedding, or Sabha).

**Course Outcomes -- CO4** **RBT Levels -- L4, L6**

### **Course Outcomes (CO)**

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

- **CO1:** Produce a high-quality dissertation that contributes original
  insights to the field of musicology.

- **CO2:** Navigate contracts, copyright laws, and digital distribution
  rights as a professional musician.

- **CO3:** Design and deliver an effective music curriculum tailored to
  different learner profiles.

- **CO4:** Demonstrate mastery over both Paper 1 (General) and Paper 2
  (Music) requirements for national eligibility.

### **Pedagogy**

- **Mentorship Model:** One-on-one supervision sessions to guide the
  dissertation process.

- **Practicum Teaching:** Supervised classroom sessions where students
  teach junior batches.

- **Professional Simulation:** Mock contract negotiations and copyright
  registration workshops.

- **Intensive Revision:** High-speed drills and retrospective analysis
  of past competitive examination papers.

## **Semester 8: Module-wise Detailed Syllabus**

#### **Module 1: The Research Dissertation (Project Work)**

- **Execution & Analysis:**

  - Refining the research question and finalizing the chapter outline.

  - Applying analytical tools (melodic/rhythmic/textual) to the research
    subject.

- **Primary & Secondary Research:** Conducting field interviews,
  analyzing recordings, and reviewing existing literature/treatises.

- **Drafting the Thesis:**

  - Writing the chapters based on the proposal approved in Semester 7.

  - **Citation & Referencing:** Strict adherence to academic styles
    (APA/Chicago) and bibliography finalization.

- **Defense Preparation:** Developing a viva-voce presentation (PPT) and
  a lecture-demonstration (Lec-Dem) to defend the research findings and
  respond to critical scholarly inquiry before a committee.

**Course Outcomes -- CO1 RBT Levels -- L6**

#### **Module 2: Professional Music Management & Law**

- **Intellectual Property (IPR):**

  - Understanding the Copyright Act (1957) in India;

  - Mechanical, performance, and synchronization rights.

- **Digital Rights Management (DRM):** Navigating streaming platforms,
  royalty collection agencies (IPRS, PPL), and social media
  monetization.

- **Grant Writing & Proposals:** How to apply for government fellowships
  (e.g., Ministry of Culture) and international arts grants.

- **Contract Law:** Basic components of artist management contracts,
  performance agreements, and recording deals.

- **Event Management:** Financial planning for concerts, grant writing
  for cultural institutions, and self-promotion strategies.

**Course Outcomes -- CO2** **RBT Levels -- L3, L4**

#### **Module 3: Advanced Pedagogy & Practical Teaching**

- **Micro-teaching:**

  - Planning and delivering 15-minute specialized modules on complex
    topics like *Vivadi Ragas* or *Tala Pranas*.

  - Methods for teaching group classes versus one-on-one *Gurukula*
    style.

- **Curriculum Design:** Designing a progressive syllabus for different
  age groups.

- **Evaluation Metrics:**

  - Creating objective and subjective assessment tools for music theory
    and practice.

  - Creating grading rubrics for practical music exams and conducting
    *Viva Voce*.

- **Psychology of Music Learning:** Identifying and addressing common
  student plateaus and performance anxiety.

**Course Outcomes -- CO3** **RBT Levels -- L3, L6**

#### **Module 4: UGC NET & JRF Intensive Review**

- **Paper 1 (General):** Final drills on Teaching Aptitude, Research
  Ethics, and Higher Education Systems.

- **Paper 2 (Music):**

  - **Units I-III:** Historical evolution, ancient treatises, and
    terminologies.

  - **Units IV-VI:** Raga-Tala systems, classification, and musical
    forms.

  - **Units VII-X:** Biographies of composers, instrumentology, and
    aesthetics

- **Critical Thinking:** Solving higher-order syllogisms and Logical
  Reasoning (Indian Logic), puzzles specific to recent exam patterns.

- **Mock Exam Strategy:** Time-management techniques for handling 150
  multiple-choice questions (MCQs).

**Course Outcomes -- CO4** **RBT Levels -- L4, L5**

### **Methodology for Semester 8:**

- **The \"Lec-Dem\" Series:** Each student must present a 30-minute
  lecture-demonstration on their dissertation topic.

- **The \"Thesis Defense\" Seminar:** A mock viva-voce where students
  present their research to a panel of external faculty.

- **Teaching Practicum:** Students are required to document 10 hours of
  supervised teaching at the undergraduate level.

- **Legal Workshop:** Drafting a sample \"No Objection Certificate\"
  (NOC) and a \"Performance Contract\" for a hypothetical concert.

- **Daily NET Drills:** Solving 20 MCQs daily with a focus on negative
  marking avoidance and logic-based elimination.

- **Portfolio Building:** Creating a professional website or digital
  profile showcasing their performance and academic work.

### **Suggested Activities**

- **The \"Copyright Challenge\":** Students bring in a song or sample
  and must determine who owns each layer of rights (Lyrics, Melody,
  Recording).

- **Syllabus Construction:** Creating a 6-month certificate course
  outline for \"Music Appreciation for Beginners.\"

- **Review Session:** Summarizing the core philosophy of a major
  treatise (like *Sangita Ratnakara*) in exactly 10 bullet points for
  quick revision.

### **Assessment Plan**

**1. Continuous Internal Evaluation (CIE) - 30%**

- **Teaching Portfolio (15%):** Submission of lesson plans and a
  self-reflection report on the teaching practicum.

- **Management Quiz (10%):** A test focused on Copyright laws and
  professional management scenarios.

- **Mock NET Result (5%):** Score achieved in a standardized internal
  Paper 1 & 2 combined test.

**2. Semester End Examination (SEE) - 70%**

- **Dissertation Submission & Viva (50 Marks):** Evaluation of the final
  thesis and the oral defense performance.

- **Final Written Exam (20 Marks):** Focused on Professional Law,
  Pedagogy, and comprehensive Musicological theory.
